# Go-to-market

Use this reference to choose an initial market, define a reachable customer, and design a measurable route to adoption.

## Frame the launch

Identify launch type, stage, target date, team, budget, existing audience, sales motion, geographic or regulatory scope, and the decision that is still open.

## Beachhead segment

Define candidate segments narrowly enough to identify real prospects.
Evaluate them on:

- severity and frequency of the problem;
- willingness and authority to pay;
- reachability through available channels;
- ability to win against alternatives;
- sales and onboarding complexity;
- reference and expansion potential;
- strategic fit.

Recommend one initial segment and name the evidence still needed.
Do not use `everyone` or a broad industry as a segment.

## Ideal Customer Profile

An ICP is a qualification rule, not a decorative persona.
Specify firmographic or contextual traits, triggering event, job to be done, current alternative, buyer, user, influencers, purchase constraints, disqualifiers, and observable qualification signals.

Distinguish the account-level ICP from user and buyer personas.

## Positioning and messaging

Build positioning from:

- specific target;
- urgent need or context;
- category or frame of reference;
- primary differentiated value;
- alternative or status quo;
- credible proof.

Then adapt messages for buyer, user, technical evaluator, and other material stakeholders.
Use the customer's language when supported by research.

## Channels and GTM motion

Compare only plausible motions: product-led, inbound, outbound, paid, community, partners, account-based, or a deliberate combination.

For each candidate, state:

- target and trigger;
- acquisition mechanism;
- activation or sales handoff;
- expected cycle and cost drivers;
- owner and required capability;
- leading metric;
- cheap validation test and stop threshold.

Do not recommend a large multi-channel plan before identifying one repeatable path.

## Growth loops

A loop must connect an action back to new or increased participation.
Map trigger, user action, produced value or artifact, exposure mechanism, recipient action, and reinvestment point.

Candidate mechanisms include referral, collaboration, usage expansion, user-generated content, and product-triggered invitations.
Distinguish a self-reinforcing loop from a linear funnel or one-time campaign.

## Launch plan

Organize work into pre-launch validation, readiness, launch window, and the first 30/60/90 days.
For each item, identify owner, dependency, evidence of completion, and metric.

Include:

- beachhead and ICP;
- positioning and proof points;
- pricing and packaging alignment;
- channel experiments;
- enablement and support readiness;
- instrumentation and feedback loops;
- risks and contingency triggers;
- adjacent segments only after beachhead evidence.

## Competitive battlecard

Use current verifiable evidence.
Cover best-fit situations, poor-fit situations, differentiated strengths, competitor strengths, traps to avoid, discovery questions, objections, proof, and win/loss signals.
Do not invent competitor weaknesses or confidential win-rate claims.

## GTM result

Lead with the chosen market and motion, then provide evidence, positioning, prioritized experiments, launch sequence, success and guardrail metrics, risks, and explicit stop or expansion criteria.
